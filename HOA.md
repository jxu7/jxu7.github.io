# Claude answer
Good questions — let's go through what the documents actually say for each.

## 1) What HOA dues include

The documents confirm most of what your agent said, with one important nuance on heating/AC:

**Confirmed in the budget/insurance docs:**
- **Water & sewer** — paid through the master association (BPCCOA), not billed to you individually
- **Garbage/recycling**
- **Cable TV & high-speed internet** (Comcast bulk agreement — renegotiated in 2026 with a 20% cost cut)
- **Natural gas** and **electricity** for common systems (this funds the building's central boiler and chiller)
- **Master property/earthquake/liability insurance**

**Heating & AC — the nuance:** Your unit has its own **fan coil unit**, which the Rules & Regs (Section 10.5) say is "the property of the homeowner," and unit owners are responsible for its **maintenance, repair, and filter changes** — at their own cost, using a licensed HVAC contractor. However, that fan coil is fed by the building's **central boiler and chiller** (common elements funded through reserves/dues), which is what actually generates the hot and chilled water/energy. So: **the "fuel" for heating/cooling is centrally provided and covered by dues, but the equipment inside your unit is your financial responsibility to maintain.** That's a meaningful distinction — if the fan coil itself breaks, that repair bill is yours, not the HOA's.

One thing **not** covered by dues: your own **in-unit electricity** (lights, outlets, appliances) — that's separately metered and billed to you.

## 2) Parking & EV charging

- **Unit 1501 is allocated Parking Space #111 on level P-1** (per the recorded Declaration/Schedule B). No storage locker is currently assigned to this unit.
- **EV charging is possible in your allocated space**, but it requires board approval through a formal process (Rules Section 22.0): you'd need to apply, confirm capacity is available in the building's EV charging circuit system, carry $500,000 in liability insurance naming the HOA as additional insured, and pay for electricity through the HOA's designated provider (Chargepoint). You'd also be responsible for installation, maintenance, and removal costs, and you'd have to disclose the charger and its terms to any future buyer.

So — yes, doable, but it's an application/approval process, not something you just install.

## 3) One year of prepaid HOA dues by the seller

**This is not mentioned anywhere in the resale certificate, and the numbers actually suggest otherwise.** Specifically:
- Section 2(b) states: *"Past due or unpaid monthly assessments against the unit total $0. There is a credit balance of $0."*

A credit balance of $0 means there's **no prepaid dues sitting on the account** for this unit — if a year of dues had been prepaid, it would typically show up here as a credit. The only prepayment mentioned in the certificate is a **standard requirement that the buyer (not the seller) deposits two months of dues into escrow at closing** — that's a normal transaction cost, not a seller-funded prepayment.

**My suggestion:** ask your agent where they got the "1 year prepaid" info — it might be a mix-up with the 2-month escrow requirement, or it could be something outside the resale certificate (like a private agreement between buyer/seller in the purchase contract, which wouldn't appear in HOA paperwork). Worth clarifying directly with the seller's agent before you rely on it.
